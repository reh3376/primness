## Observations

- The algebraic side is smoother than the raw Dickman prediction at every bound. The ratio observed/predicted runs from 1.76 at B = 2^20 to 2.87 at B = 2^14; it grows as B shrinks and u grows.
- The size shift that would reconcile Dickman with the algebraic count (implied α) is -4.08 to -3.76 across the four bounds, mean -3.93, spread 0.32. A single shift explains all four bounds to within that spread, which is what a root-property correction would look like. CADO's comment line for this polynomial reports α = −3.96; that number was not used anywhere in the measurement.
- The rational side is less smooth than Dickman at every bound; its implied α is +1.16 to +1.24. A linear form over coprime pairs is not a random integer of its size, and the correction has the opposite sign.
- Within B = 2^20, binned by u in steps of 0.1 (bins with at least 1,000 values): the excess ratio is 1.44 at u ≈ 2.1 and 1.83 at u ≈ 3.4. The excess is not a constant factor; it rises with u, as a shift in ln|F| predicts, because ρ falls faster at larger u.
- Both sides smooth at B = 2^20: 110,465 values, a fraction of 0.02018, against 0.02045 if the two sides were independent (ratio 0.99). The sides are close to independent.
- Algebraic density per b row at B = 2^20: mean over the first quarter of b (1..375) is 0.09122, over the last quarter (1126..1500) is 0.04309. Larger b means larger values, so density falls across the region; the comparison with Dickman already accounts for size value by value.
- 94.1% of algebraic values have a prime factor above 2^20. Of the smooth ones, the largest prime factor sits mostly in the top few octaves below B (see `lpf_hist`), as expected for values far larger than B.

- Does the harness reproduce a Dickman-scale smoothness count on a real NFS polynomial? Yes: predicted densities are of the right order at all four bounds, and the counts are far above Poisson noise.
