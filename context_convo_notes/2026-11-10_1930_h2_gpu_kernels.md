## Why

H1 measured the 200-digit relation collection on the CPU: 3,896 laptop-hours, of which 87 to 89 percent is the bucket sieve and the ECM cofactorization. The success criterion counts the laptop's GPU, so the GPU had to be measured on those two kernels before the laptop-plus-GPU envelope could be called insufficient.

## What was built

- `docs/h2_falsifiable_prediction.md`, committed alone in d183e59 before any GPU number. H2 holds if the GPU is at most 10 times the 18 CPU cores on each kernel.
- `h2-cpu` (Rust). With `--prepare` it writes the shared inputs: 7,600,041 primes in (2^15, 2^27] with roots, and 131,072 Montgomery lanes with 96-bit moduli, whose constants come from num-bigint. With `--run` it times the CPU kernels: the bucket sieve of one 2^29 line with 18 threads and per-thread buckets of 2^16, Montgomery multiplication with two 64-bit limbs over eight interleaved chains per thread, and a 1 GiB copy. Tests check the chain against num-bigint and the lane file round trip.
- `tools/h2_gpu/main.swift` (Metal, shaders compiled at runtime). It runs the same kernels on the GPU: a fill through atomic bucket counters in device memory, an apply in threadgroup memory with 16-bit counters, three 32-bit limbs for the multiplication, and a copy.
- `scripts/h2_run.sh` runs both sides under `hw-sample`. `scripts/h2_summary.py` takes the medians, cross-checks the two sides and applies the criterion.

## What was measured

| kernel | CPU | GPU | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| bucket sieve (updates/s) | 2.61e9 | 2.15e10 | 8.2 |
| Montgomery mod 96-bit n (per s) | 4.14e9 | 8.10e10 | 19.6 |
| copy (bytes/s) | 3.54e11 | 5.42e11 | 1.5 |

- The sieve checksum is identical on both sides (6,282,484,675), and all 18,432 shared lanes agree.
- H2 is rejected on the multiplication.
- The bound the protocol fixes is 3,896 / 20.6 = 189 laptop-hours with the GPU. Applying each kernel's own ratio to H1's time shares gives 350 to 755 laptop-hours.
- A manual run before the scripted one gave 7.4 and 18.4.

## Questions answered

- Does the GPU bring the laptop near one hour at 200 digits? No: at least 189 hours for the relations alone, and more likely 350 to 755. Obstacle H is forced on the primary hardware envelope.
- Where is the GPU strong? Word arithmetic, about 20 times the CPU. A port would move the cofactorization first.

## Questions raised

- H3: what a machine built for the two kernels would have to do per second at one hour. That is 8,300 special-q's, 4.5 × 10^12 sieve positions and about 4 × 10^7 cofactorizations, with the linear algebra still to be measured.
- How much a tuned 96-bit multiplication on the CPU, and a real two-dimensional lattice siever on the GPU, would move the ratios.

## Hardware

Each side's run takes a few seconds. The CPU side averaged 55.5 W with the CPU at 67.0 °C at most. The GPU side averaged 59.6 W with the GPU fully active and at 56.4 °C at most.
