## Why

H5 showed that a perfect relation collection would leave the rest of the factorization: the linear algebra and polynomial selection. The roadmap had carried those as the records' 9 to 17 percent share, about 400 to 800 laptop-hours. The linear algebra at 200 digits had never been measured on this machine, and H3's rates also need it.

## What was built

- `docs/h6_falsifiable_prediction.md`, committed alone in 2fc8e2e before any timing. H6 holds if block Wiedemann at 200 digits takes between 23 and 92 laptop-hours.
- `h6-matrix` (Rust, three tests) writes synthetic matrices in CADO-NFS's binary format.
  - Row weights are gamma-distributed with RSA-130's ratio of spread to mean.
  - Columns follow P(i) ∝ (i + 32)^-0.94, the NFS profile in CADO-NFS's `random_matrix` source. The standalone tool draws uniformly.
- `scripts/h6_linalg.sh` does three things:
  - re-merges RSA-130 at density 170 for κ;
  - runs bwc's `prep` and `krylov` (thr=3x6, m = n = 64) on the real RSA-130 matrix, its synthetic twin, and synthetic matrices at a quarter, a half and all of the 200-digit size;
  - runs `lingen_b64` on random input of the 200-digit length.
- `scripts/h6_summary.py` gives the per-interval times with the machine's activity, ρ and the gate, the total and the criterion.

## What was measured

| matrix | rows | s per iteration | ns per nonzero |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RSA-130, real | 1,183,823 | 0.0108 | 0.082 |
| synthetic, RSA-130's shape | 1,183,823 | 0.0120 | 0.091 |
| synthetic, 200 digits | 14,107,489 | 0.2676 | 0.112 |

- κ = 0.849, N_200 = 14,107,489, ρ = 0.897. The calibration gate passed at 11.5 percent.
- T_LA = (661,289 × 0.240 s + 3,416 s of lingen) × 1.064 = **47.9 laptop-hours**, so H6 holds.
- Counting clean intervals only gives 55.8 hours, and the top of H4's size range gives 62.5.

## Questions answered

- How long is the linear algebra at 200 digits? 48 to 63 laptop-hours, a sixth to an eighth of the records'-share stand-in.
- What would a perfect relation collection leave? At least 48 hours plus polynomial selection: at most 63 to 82 times faster before polynomial selection, about 15 times if it kept its 5 to 6 percent share.

## Questions raised

- Polynomial selection at 200 digits: the one part of the cost not measured.
- The products are memory-bound, at 32 W. Whether the GPU, with 1.5 times the CPU's copy bandwidth in H2, would speed them up is not tested.

## Hardware

The 200-digit `krylov` ran for 27 minutes with the CPU at 89 °C at most. In clean intervals the CPU drew 32 to 33 W, with the tiers at 3.3 and 2.6 GHz. Other work on the machine, visible as GPU activity, slowed six of the eleven intervals after the first. It also slowed the synthetic twin's last three calibration intervals, which is why the clean-interval ρ, 1.122, differs from the protocol's.
