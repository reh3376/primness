## Why

H4 put the sieving phase at 60 to 77 percent of a complete factorization's wall time, rising with size. The owner asked whether a more efficient sieve, or something that replaces it, is where the work should go. That phase holds both the sieve and the ECM cofactorization of its survivors. No change confined to the sieve can save more than the sieve's own share.

## What was built

- `docs/h5_falsifiable_prediction.md`, committed alone in 2f1ab98 before any categorized timing at 130 digits. H5 holds if the sieve proper is under two thirds of RSA-130's wall time, which is a ceiling below 3 times.
- `scripts/h5_sieve_share.sh` reuses the polynomial, factor base and client parameters of H4's RSA-130 run.
  - Nine `las -t 2 -random-sample 300 -T` processes run at once, one per ninth of the run's special-q range, under `hw-sample`.
  - It counts the run's special-q's per workunit for the weights.
  - It keeps `las`'s set-up lines and closing statistics.
- `scripts/h5_summary.py` computes the weighted split, s, the ceilings and the criterion. It also gives H1's split at 200 digits and where the cost grows from 130 to 200 digits.

## What was measured

| group | share of `las`'s time at RSA-130 |
|---|---:|
| sieve proper (norms 1.2%, sieving 77.5%, survivor search 6.2%) | 84.9% |
| cofactorization | 10.8% |
| bookkeeping | 4.3% |

- s = 0.849 × 0.767 = 0.651. A free sieve buys at most 2.87 times at the reach, so H5 holds, narrowly. The derivation predicted 1.7 to 2.2 times by carrying H1's 200-digit split down. At 130 digits the cofactorization's share is a third of what it is at 200.
- At 200 digits a free sieve buys 1.8 to 2.7 times, and a free relation collection 5.9 to 11 times.
- From RSA-130 to 200 digits:
  - relations needed: 14.0×;
  - positions per relation: 144×;
  - positions in all: 2,015×;
  - core-nanoseconds per position: 1.45× (9.8 to 14.1);
  - `las` core-seconds: 2,913×.

## Questions answered

- Is the sieve where a large speedup can come from? Not while it examines the same candidates. A perfect sieve buys under 3 times at the reach and under 3 times at 200 digits. One hour at 200 digits needs about 4,500 times.
- Why does the cost grow? The cost per candidate barely moves. The number of candidates grows with how rarely a value is smooth, and that is set by the values' size.

## Questions raised

- The linear algebra at 200 digits: the floor that a perfect sieve, or a perfect relation collection, would leave.
- At the goal's size the cofactorization is 25 to 37 percent of `las`'s time, against 11 percent at the reach. How much of it a GPU ECM takes off is H2's open question.

## Hardware

The nine samples took 51 seconds, with the CPU 99 percent active at 58 W and the tiers at 4.1 and 3.7 GHz. Those are near the clocks of H1's samples. Only shares enter the statistic.
